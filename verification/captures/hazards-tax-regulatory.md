## CAL FIRE / Office of the State Fire Marshal — Fire Hazard Severity Zones
- URL: https://osfm.fire.ca.gov/what-we-do/community-wildfire-preparedness-and-mitigation/fire-hazard-severity-zones
- Retrieved: 2026-11-07.

Published facts used:
- FHSZ classifications in State Responsibility Area are exactly three: **Moderate, High, Very High**.
- "Fire Hazard Severity Zone maps evaluate 'hazard', not 'risk'." Hazard is assessed over "a 30 to 50-year
  period without considering mitigation measures."
- The Office completed State Responsibility Area mapping at end of 2023, effective April 2024; Local
  Responsibility Area maps were rolled out to counties through 2025 (phases Feb–Mar 2025).
- For a property inside a Local Responsibility Area (a city, such as Los Angeles), OSFM directs the owner to
  "Contact your local jurisdiction."

Consequence recorded on the site: a parcel-level FHSZ rating for a City of Los Angeles site **cannot be
assigned** from these pages without an address; the value stays blank and is flagged.

## California Geological Survey — Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zones
- URL: https://www.conservation.ca.gov/cgs/alquist-priolo
- Retrieved: 2026-11-07.

Published facts used:
- "Wherever an active fault exists, if it has the potential for surface rupture, a structure for human
  occupancy cannot be placed over the fault and must be a minimum distance from the fault (generally fifty feet)."
- "An active fault, for the purposes of the Alquist-Priolo Act, is one that has ruptured in the last 11,000 years."
- The Act addresses only surface fault rupture; liquefaction, landslide, amplified shaking and tsunami/seiche
  are covered by the Seismic Hazards Mapping Act.
- Sellers must disclose fault-zone location to buyers (Civil Code §1103).

Consequence recorded on the site: a 50-foot setback is real site-constraint risk (it can sterilise part of a
small parcel), but whether a 1,372 sq ft prototype with a drive-thru lane can be sited legally depends on the
actual parcel, which is unknown.

## California Department of Insurance — FAIR Plan modernisation (release 031-2024)
- URL: https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0100-press-releases/2024/release031-2024.cfm
- Released July 26, 2024. Retrieved 2026-11-07.
- "Establishing a new 'high-value' commercial coverage option with limits up to $20 million per building."
- The FAIR Plan is described as the state's "insurer of last resort", and the release states that a growing
  FAIR Plan "causes traditional insurance companies to withdraw from certain areas."

## Los Angeles County Assessor — Decline-in-Value (Proposition 8)
- URL: https://assessor.lacounty.gov/tax-relief/decline-in-value
- Retrieved: 2026-11-07.
- "California's Proposition 13 established the base year value for property tax assessment. It also caps the
  growth of a property's assessed value at no more than two percent a year unless the market value of a
  property falls below the base year value."
- Proposition 8 "allows a temporary reduction in assessed value when a property suffers a 'Decline-in-Value'."
- Filing window: **July 2 through November 30**, form **RP-87**, for the fiscal year beginning July 1.

## City of Los Angeles Office of Finance — Real Property Transfer Tax and Measure ULA FAQ
- URL: https://finance.lacity.gov/faq/measure-ula
- Retrieved: 2026-11-07.

| Value of property conveyed | Base rate | ULA rate | Applicable tax rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| > $100; ≤ $5,400,000 | $2.25 / $500 | 0% | 0.45% |
| > $5,400,000; < $10,900,000 | $2.25 / $500 | 4% | 4.45% |
| ≥ $10,900,000 | $2.25 / $500 | 5.5% | 5.95% |

Additional published statements:
- "Effective for transactions closing after June 30, 2026, the new ULA thresholds will be $5,400,000 and $10,900,000."
- "the value thresholds of when to apply the ULA Tax and its corresponding rates are adjusted annually based on
  the Bureau of Labor Statistics Chained Consumer Price Index."
- The ULA Tax "is applicable to qualified conveyances of real property interests that occur on or after April 1, 2023."

about homelessness programmes and does not contain the rates. The rates above come from the Office of
Finance page, which does. The earlier citation is retained in this capture only as a record of the defect.

## California Department of Industrial Relations — AB 1228 / Fast Food Council
- URL: https://www.dir.ca.gov/AB1228/AB1228.html
- Retrieved: 2026-11-07.
- "raises the hourly minimum wage rate for certain fast food workers to $20 effective April 1, 2024."
- The DIR page also states a Fast Food Council was established "to establish an hourly minimum wage for fast
  food restaurant employees" — i.e. the $20 floor is a floor, not a ceiling, and can be raised by the Council.

**Correction recorded:** an earlier build cited this DIR page for a "City of Los Angeles minimum wage (2026)"
row. The DIR page is about the *state* fast-food wage under AB 1228; it does not publish a City of Los Angeles
minimum wage. That row was blanked (it was already blank) and re-pointed to an LA-specific official source.
