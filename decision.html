Decision

Category 7 · the recommendation

What to do with this property

One asset, one tenant, one expiry window, one owner whose wealth is concentrated. The recommendation below is built to survive bad luck, not to maximise a spreadsheet.

Recommended course of action

Renew early into a long term, then sell into that term — and start now, not in 2030.

Selling before expiry and renewing are not opposites; they are sequential steps in the same plan. The value of a single-tenant NNN asset is its remaining lease term. So the highest-value sequence is: create term at a market rent, then sell the term to a buyer who can finance it.

If the tenant will not sign a market renewal, the plan changes immediately and without sentiment: market the property while at least three years of term remain. What must never happen is the third option — drifting to expiry with no signed renewal and no buyer — because at that point the asset is priced as vacant land and the Measure ULA tax becomes an unavoidable cost on a damaged price.

The one number that can overturn this

Everything depends on the cap rate a buyer applies today. If the achievable cap on a short-lease asset is 6.5% rather than 7.0%, selling now is worth 14.46× and holding needs a 75.5% renewal probability to compete. If it is 7.5%, the bar drops to 45.4%. That is a 30-point swing in the decision threshold produced by a 100bp pricing difference — which is why the first spend of money in this plan is an appraisal and a broker opinion of value, not a lawyer.

Timeline

Q4 2026 – Q1 2027 — close the information gaps. Pull the lease and amendments, the guaranty, the estoppel, the insurance declarations, the property tax bill, and the parcel hazard lookups (U-01, U-02). Order an appraisal and a broker opinion of value. Nothing is signed before this is done.
2027 — test the tenant's intention in writing. Ask for a renewal proposal and, directly, whether this unit is on the retention list. Put the question in front of the franchisee and the franchisor; the franchisor's consent matters even when it is not the payer.
2028 — the decision year. If a market renewal exists, sign it (strategy C) and begin preparing the asset for sale. If it does not exist, list the property with at least three years of term remaining.
2028–2029 — exploit the events. The Los Angeles market has a demand peak scheduled in 2028; a stabilised, long-lease asset should be marketed into it, not after it. Confirm any event-specific assumptions independently before relying on them.
2029–2031 — execute the exit or hold the new lease. If sold, complete the exchange or diversification inside the statutory window. If held, the asset is now a long-lease bond and the decision cadence drops to annual review.

Wealth preservation plan for the individual

Concentration is the biggest risk you own

The largest exposure on this site is not the tenant, the cap rate or the fault line — it is that one lease, one building and one seismic region may be most of your net worth. Before optimising the sale price, set a hard ceiling on the share of net worth this asset may represent and work towards it deliberately. The risk register treats that as RSK-15.

Exit mechanics worth more than a rent negotiation

  • Exchange, don't just sell. A like-kind exchange defers federal capital-gains and depreciation-recapture tax; it must be set up before closing (qualified intermediary, strict deadlines). Confirm with tax counsel — this site does not give tax advice.
  • Installment / structured sale. Spreading gain over years can reduce bracket compression and, in some structures, the ULA hit — model it before listing, not after.
  • Price the ULA in from day one. 4.0% (or 5.5% above $10m) of the full price is a seller cost in the City of Los Angeles. A $5m sale loses $200,000 to it before any other cost.
  • Do not personally guarantee. No new recourse, no cross-collateralisation, no guarantee of a franchisee's obligation.

Liquidity rule

Hold 12–24 months of carrying costs and personal living expenses outside this asset. The purpose is not return; it is to prevent a forced sale. A forced sale in a wide-cap market, on a short lease, pays the ULA tax on a depressed price — the worst of all outcomes.

Diversify across tenants, geographies and hazards

If the proceeds are reinvested, spread them: different tenants, different metros, different seismic and wildfire zones, and a mix of lease lengths. A diversified NNN portfolio or a DST interest can hold the income profile without re-concentrating the risk. Size each position so that no single vacancy matters.

What would change this recommendation

  • A corporate lease with a strong guaranty. If the payer on this lease is Jack in the Box Inc. itself rather than a franchisee, the credit is materially better and strategy C becomes clearly dominant instead of merely preferred.
  • Anchored, long-lease, high-growth submarket. If the parcel is in a corridor with verified transit investment and rising household income — not measurable without the address — holding to redevelop rather than sell could outperform.
  • A cap-rate environment below 6%. If term is cheap, holding and refinancing becomes rational again.
  • Documented, insurable, retrofitted building. Removes the tail-risk discount that currently pushes towards exit.
Three things this analysis cannot tell you

1. What the property is actually worth today (no appraisal). 2. What the lease actually says (no lease). 3. What the parcel-level hazard exposure is (no address). The recommendation is deliberately built to be robust to those gaps — renew-then-sell works across the assumptions, and the fallback is triggered by information, not by hope. But it is not a substitute for the three documents that would close them.
