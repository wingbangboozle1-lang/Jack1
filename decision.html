Analysis · decision

Verdict: renew on market terms, hold, sell on a trigger

Renew, hold, and let a priced bid - not a hunch - decide the sale.

Five to nine years of remaining term is the asset's main value. Everything verified here points the same way: keep the contractual income, negotiate from evidence, and refuse to sell into a number nobody has supported. The risks that would change this answer are named, with thresholds, below.

Risk balance

Wealth preservation versus growth

What holding protects

  • Contractual NNN rent and the tax treatment of an income asset, with no forced sale date.
  • Optionality: a re-based rent at renewal, a re-tenanting play at expiry, or a land-value exit.
  • No transfer tax now. At a $5.4m price the city tax alone is $24,300; at $5.4m+1 it is $240,300.
  • Section 1031 remains available later: 45 days to identify and 180 days to close once you do sell (REG-09).

What holding costs

  • Concentration: one tenant, one parcel, one insurance market.
  • Credit drift: systemwide same-store sales of -7.4% in Q4 FY2025 and 86 closures in the year.
  • Cost inflation the lease may not fully pass through: Los Angeles CPI +3.7%, energy +20.3% (PRI-01, PRI-06).
  • Rate risk: a widening from 5.25% to 7.25% cuts value by roughly 28% at constant NOI (see the model grid).

How to keep the risk manageable while keeping the upside: sign the renewal with escalations and a guaranty (raises the numerator), keep or obtain insurance that actually responds to fire and quake (removes the uninsurable tail), hold a capital reserve sized to a realistic re-leasing period (absorbs the vacancy scenario), and set the sale decision in advance with the triggers above (removes the emotional sale). None of those steps requires knowing today's capitalisation rate; all of them improve what a buyer will pay if you do sell.

Next 90 days

The checklist

  1. Pull the executed lease and all amendments; calendar every renewal notice date. If the notice window has already passed, get written confirmation of the tenant's intention.
  2. Order title, survey and the assessor's parcel record; confirm zoning and legal use in ZIMAS.
  3. Request the tenant's last eight quarters of unit-level sales and its franchisee financials if the operator is a franchisee.
  4. Obtain three closed net-lease comparables and two rent comparables for central Los Angeles; derive a cap-rate range.
  5. Get insurance quotes for fire, quake and business interruption; compare with what the lease requires the tenant to carry.
  6. Take the renewal proposal and the comps to a tax adviser; model the sale under Section 1031 and without it.
  7. Write the trigger table above into your file, with the dates you will re-check each series.
