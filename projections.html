Projections & model

The only official projection is a demographic one — and it points down.

This page separates three things that are usually blurred together: what the State of California actually projects, what this brief calculates from it, and what the scenario model assumes. The first is a fact, the second is arithmetic on that fact, and the third is an assumption set that the reader can see and challenge.

Scenario model

Hold versus sell, in index units

Risk-free rate basis ↗

No rent, price or cap rate for this asset is public, so the model runs in index units: one year of current rent = 100 units. Every output is a ratio, which is valid for comparing paths and invalid as a valuation. It answers one question: how much has to be assumed for the hold case to beat the sell case?

The honest break-even On the base assumptions the hold case needs the exit multiple in 2035 to be at least 7.4× the year-9 rent for the hold present value to match the net proceeds of selling today. That is a plausible multiple, which is why the answer is not obvious — and why the recommendation is to keep both paths open rather than to commit to either on the current data. 61% of the hold case's present value comes from the residual, so the exit assumption dominates the result. An owner who believes the residual will be weak should sell.

Transparency

Every model assumption, named

These are assumptions, not facts. Each one is a lever; change it and the answer moves.
RefAssumptionValue usedWhy
A-01Holding period used to compare hold versus sell9 years (to 2035, the outer end of the stated expiry window)Compares the two paths over the longest possible remaining term. A shorter term shortens the hold case's advantage.
A-02Rent growth at renewal0% to +10% in equal stepsFixed bumps are typical but the actual structure is unknown (PR-09). The range spans a flat renewal to a modest increase.
A-03Discount rate applied to the rent stream6%, 7%, 8%A sensitivity band around a risk-free rate of 4.84% (C-01) plus a net-lease risk premium. These are illustrative rates, not a market quote.
A-04Residual value at the end of the holding period15 times the then-current rentA 6.67% exit cap as a round proxy. This is the single most influential assumption in the model and it is an assumption, not a verified cap rate.
A-05Illustrative rent base100 units of indexThe model runs in index units, not dollars, because no rent was disclosed (PR-08). Every output is therefore a ratio, which is valid for comparing paths and invalid as a valuation.
A-06Transfer tax on sale4.45% of price (lower ULA band, X-06)Chosen because a central-LA drive-thru parcel most plausibly trades in the $5.4m-$10.9m band. If it trades below $5.4m the rate is 0.45%; if above $10.9m it is 5.95%.
A-08Selling commission4% of priceAn assumed transaction cost, not a market quote. It is added to the published transfer tax to reach net proceeds.
A-07Binary event probabilitiesNot assignedThe brief deliberately assigns no probability to wildfire, earthquake, flood or recession for this parcel. Assigning one would require data that does not exist at parcel level, and any number would be fabricated.
