Area economy · population, income and housing

Geography: city and county - never the parcel

A declining county population with rising incomes and very high housing costs.

Los Angeles County lost 3.2% of its population between the 2020 estimate base and July 2025, and the city lost 0.8%. Incomes and housing costs still rose. Both facts matter for a restaurant site: they describe the customer base and the labour pool, and they conflict, so neither can be used alone.

Reading: median gross rent is $1,933 a month in the city and $1,954 in the county, with a 36.0% owner-occupied rate in the city. High housing cost absorbs household budget before discretionary food spend, which is one mechanism behind the tenant's negative same-store sales. It also constrains hiring: the California fast-food minimum wage is $20.00 an hour (see regulation). County building permits of 22,250 in 2025 show construction is active, which supports construction employment but also spreads demand across new locations.
