Risk register

What can go wrong, how bad it is, and what to do about it.

Likelihood and severity are analyst judgements, stated openly — official statistics do not publish the probability that a specific franchisee walks away from a specific lease. What is verifiable, and is therefore cited, is the evidence underneath each judgement.

Black-swan events

The brief asks specifically about events that could destroy the local economy. The honest treatment is to separate severity, which can be reasoned about, from probability, which official data does not support at parcel level. Nothing below assigns a fabricated probability.

Wildfire

Structure: a single-storey drive-thru box, low fuel load, urban block — materially lower direct fire risk than a hillside asset. The real exposure is indirect: insurance repricing, buyer sentiment, and evacuation-level disruption to a trade area. The January 2025 fires cost the FAIR Plan alone about $3.5 billion across roughly 5,400 claims.

Severity: high, metro-wide. Parcel-level hazard: cannot be determined without the address — flagged, not assumed.

Earthquake

The LA basin sits over blind-thrust faults. A single-storey wood-and-steel box is one of the more survivable structures, but business interruption is not: an unsafe-to-occupy placard can stop the restaurant for months regardless of physical damage.

Severity: very high. Mitigation: confirm the lease allocates seismic risk, and confirm insurance actually covers business interruption for the tenant.

Insurance-market failure

The FAIR Plan now carries $724 billion of exposure and 21,717 commercial policies, with statutory rights to assess admitted insurers when claims outrun its capacity. If the residual market is the only market, the cost of cover becomes a tax on occupancy — and a tenant facing that tax is a tenant that recalculates rent.

Severity: high. Signal to watch: who carries the policy at renewal, and whether the premium is inside the tenant's controllable-cost cap.

Tenant insolvency

A franchisee bankruptcy can reject a lease in a way a corporate tenant's would not, and the brand's own disclosure names rising bad debt. In that scenario the landlord becomes an unsecured creditor for pre-petition rent.

Severity: high. Mitigation: know who the tenant of record is, and whether there is a guaranty. Both are withheld in this file — see flagged items.

Rate shock

Not rare, just slow. The verified 10-year at 4.90% against a 3.75–4.00% policy rate is already the post-2022 regime. A further 100bp would take the short-term cap materially above 8%, which is a double-digit percentage hit to value.

Severity: high. Mitigation: sell into a market you chose, on a timetable you set — not at a forced expiry.

Regulatory shock

Local transfer taxes are indexed upward annually by statute, and the state wage floor ratchets with CPI-W. A future expansion of Measure ULA, or commercial rent regulation, would reprice exit economics overnight.

Severity: medium. Note: the ULA threshold is now $5.4M and rises with the Chained CPI — a sale above it pays 4% of the whole price.
