Projection Model

Category 6 · the projection system

A projection model that refuses to invent the property

The rent, the price, the assessed value and the cap rate are all unknown or private. So the model is built in units of one year of current base rent. Every output is a multiple. Multiply by the real rent when you have it — the structure of the answer does not change.

Why this design

It is easy to produce a confident-looking dollar valuation from invented inputs. That is what most property "analyses" do. This one separates the two things cleanly: verified facts (Treasury yield, ULA rate, tenant trajectory, population projection) and transparent assumptions (cap rates, renewal probability, dark value). Only the second group is adjustable, and every one of them is listed with its reason.

The three strategies

A · Sell now (2027)

Capitalise current rent at a wider cap rate, because a buyer is taking a lease with about six years left. Net of exit cost (~6%: ULA 4.0% verified plus assumptions).

B · Hold to expiry

Collect contractual rent to expiry, then face the branch: the tenant renews (probability p) and the asset is sold on a fresh lease, or the tenant leaves and the asset is worth vacant/redevelopment value.

C · Renew early, then sell

Convert uncertainty into term in 2028 by signing a long renewal at a market reset, then sell in ~2032 with double-digit years remaining — a bigger buyer pool and a tighter cap.

What the model is not

  • It is not an appraisal and does not produce a price. A licensed appraiser and a broker opinion of value do that.
  • It does not model local rent comparables, because none were verified for this parcel.
  • It does not model tax on sale (federal/state capital gains, depreciation recapture) — that requires the owner's basis, which is private. ULA is included because it is a per-transaction statutory cost.
  • It does not assume the tenant's credit improves. That would be an inference, not a fact.
