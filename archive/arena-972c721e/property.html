The property

An anonymised dossier: what is known, what is withheld, and the lease clock that actually forces the decision.

What is actually known

  • Asset type: single-tenant quick-service restaurant building on a net (NNN) lease.
  • Tenant brand: Jack in the Box (NASDAQ: JACK — verified via SEC filings).
  • Location granularity: "around Central Los Angeles" — the owner's own wording. Not narrowed further, by design.
  • Lease expiry: somewhere in 2030–2035 — the owner's own wording. Presented as three cases, not one number.
  • Decision to be made: sell before expiry, or renew and hold.

The lease clock

A net lease does not end on the expiry date. It ends when the renewal option lapses, which is why the notice window — not the expiry date — is the decision deadline.

  1. TodayLease in force. Remaining term is somewhere between 4 and 9 years, because the expiry sits somewhere in a five-year window. The owner has not supplied the exact date.
  2. Notice windowTypically 6–12 months before expiry in a net lease. This, not the expiry date, is the real decision deadline — miss it and the tenant's option lapses and the asset is priced as a vacancy risk.
  3. 2030–2035Lease expiry, per the owner's own description. At expiry the tenant either renews, renegotiates, or leaves.
  4. Today to 2035The investment horizon used throughout the model.

What is withheld, and why

The brief requires that no personal information appears in the product. A property address is personal information: it identifies the owner. So the address, parcel number, rent, NOI, tenant of record and lease document are all withheld, and every analysis below is either area-level (public data) or explicitly labelled as an assumption band.

The consequence you should weigh: the decision framework is complete, but the final numbers are not. The model runs on three illustrative NOI cases. Replace them with your actual NOI and the same engine gives you your actual answer — that is what the Valuation page and its calculator are for.
