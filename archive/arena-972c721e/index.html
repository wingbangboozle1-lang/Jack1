Decision brief · built 2026-11-06

Sell the Jack in the Box before the lease expires, or renew and hold?

Renew — but renew now, and do not sell into the expiry. The single most valuable thing in this file is time: a buyer pays roughly 20.8% more for the same net operating income when nine years of lease term remain instead of three. Waiting until the lease is nearly over is the one path the model shows destroying value in every expiry case, and it is also the path that requires no decision at all.

The recommended sequence is: negotiate a long extension now (while the tenant still wants the site), hold about five years to keep the rent and the Proposition 13 tax wedge, then sell with five years of firm term into the deepest buyer pool. If the tenant refuses to extend, the answer flips: sell within twelve months rather than ride the lease to expiry.

The three findings that drive the answer

1 · Term is the price

Cap rates are a function of how long the buyer can count on the rent. In this model 3 years of term prices at 7.25%, five years at 6.5%, nine years at 6.0%. On unchanged income that is a 11.5% step from three to five years and 20.8% from three to nine.

See the term curve →

2 · The tenant is contracting

Jack in the Box Inc. reported system same-store sales of −1.1%, guided to 50–60 closures in fiscal 2026 — "most of which will be franchise restaurants" — and folds "block closure" and "selling real estate" into its own turnaround plan. A renewal is not something to assume; it is something to negotiate while you still have something to trade.

Read the SEC record →

3 · The area is full, but not growing

Los Angeles unemployment is 4.4% — effectively full employment — while total nonfarm jobs grew 0.5% year over year and the city's population is down 0.8% since 2020 (the county, −3.2%). Demand is deep but static, and the local wage floor is the highest in the country for this format.

Where the risk sits

Black-swan surface

Wildfire, earthquake, and the insurance channel that links them. California's FAIR Plan now carries 21,717 commercial policies (from 7,315 in 2022) and $724 billion of exposure. A net lease passes insurance cost to the tenant — which protects the owner's income but raises the tenant's occupancy cost, and therefore the odds it walks.
