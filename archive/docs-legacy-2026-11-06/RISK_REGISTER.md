# Risk register — SP-01

Likelihood and severity are analyst judgements, stated openly. Official statistics do not publish the
probability that a specific franchisee leaves a specific lease. The evidence beneath each judgement is
cited; the judgement is not dressed up as data.

| Risk | Likelihood | Severity | Evidence | Management |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tenant non-renewal | High | High | JACK Q3 FY2026: 17 closures vs 4 openings; FY2026 guidance 50–60 closures, "most of which will be franchise restaurants"; franchise margin 39.3% → 37.4%; bad debt higher; "block closure" and "selling real estate" named in the turnaround | Negotiate the extension now; if refused, sell within twelve months |
| Lease-tail pricing cliff | High | High | Term premium in the model: +11.5% from 3 to 5 years, +20.8% from 3 to 9 years | Never market the asset with a short tail |
| Interest-rate shock | Medium | High | 10-year Treasury 4.90% vs 3.75–4.00% funds range — a +90bp term spread; ±75bp is worth roughly 18 months of rent at this size | Model the band; sell on your timetable, not a forced expiry |
| Insurance repricing | High | Medium | FAIR Plan commercial policies 7,315 (2022) → 21,717 (Dec 2025); total exposure $724bn; ~$3.5bn paid on ~5,400 Eaton/Palisades claims | Establish who insures and who pays; in a true NNN the tenant absorbs it, which raises its occupancy cost and its incentive to leave |
| Wildfire (indirect) | Medium | High | CAL FIRE FHSZ classes Moderate/High/Very High; LRA determinations come from the local jurisdiction; 2026 YTD statewide 6,389 fires / 339,481 acres / 177 structures | Parcel FHSZ cannot be determined without the address — flagged. Treat metro-wide insurance repricing as the live exposure |
| Earthquake | Low | Very high | USGS 2023 50-state National Seismic Hazard Model; LA basin; parcel site class and liquefaction not determinable without the address | Seismic and insurance review before transacting; confirm the lease allocates seismic risk and that business interruption is covered |
| Measure ULA threshold | Low | Medium | 4% of the whole price above $5,400,000; 5.5% at/above $10,900,000; ~$216,000 at the threshold | If value sits near the line, price with the cliff in view — never structure falsely around it |
| Exit tax | Certain | Medium | 0.56% city+county transfer tax; ~5.0% brokerage and closing; capital gains on a private basis; 1031 defers but does not eliminate (45/180 days) | Brief a CPA and qualified intermediary before closing |
| Concentration | High | High | One parcel, one tenant, one sub-market, one metro | The cheapest fix is a 1031 into diversified net-lease assets at exit |
| Policy / regulatory | Medium | Medium | Fast-food minimum wage $20.00/hr (above the $16.90 state rate), Council can raise it, state rate indexed to CPI-W capped at +3.5%; ULA thresholds indexed annually | Build the wage floor into renewal rent expectations rather than resisting it |
| Execution risk on the negotiation | Medium | Medium | A concession of roughly $150k is modelled; the tenant may demand more or refuse | Set a walk-away: if the extension costs more than the term premium is worth, sell |

## Black-swan events

| Event | Why it is on the list | What would change the value | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wildfire | January 2025 repriced insurance and sentiment across the metro, not just in the burn areas | Insurance cost, buyer pool, trade-area disruption | Not asserted — official data does not support parcel-level probability |
| Earthquake | LA basin blind-thrust faulting | Physical damage plus months of business interruption even without structural loss | Not asserted |
| Insurance-market failure | FAIR Plan at $724bn exposure with statutory assessment rights | Occupancy cost becomes a tax; a tenant facing it recalculates rent | Not asserted |
| Tenant insolvency | Rising bad debt disclosed by the franchisor | Lease rejection; landlord becomes unsecured creditor for pre-petition rent | Not asserted |
| Rate shock | 4.90% 10-year already restricts valuations | +100bp takes short-term caps above 8% — a double-digit value hit | Not asserted |
| Regulatory shock | ULA thresholds indexed upward annually; state wage floor ratchets | Exit economics and tenant economics both shift | Not asserted |

**A note on refusing to quantify.** It would be easy to write "a 2% annual chance of a major earthquake".
That number would be invented. The honest structure is: severity reasoned from official data, probability
left to the reader's own risk tolerance, and the decision framed so it survives either.
