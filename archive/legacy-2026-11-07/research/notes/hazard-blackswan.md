## Source M — Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zones (California Geological Survey)
URL: https://www.conservation.ca.gov/cgs/alquist-priolo
Verified: regulatory zones around surface traces of active faults; "a structure for human occupancy cannot be placed over the fault and must be a minimum distance from the fault (generally **fifty feet**)"; active fault defined as rupture in the last 11,000 years; zones exist in **37 counties** affecting **more than 100 cities**; CGS notes "Alquist-Priolo Act only addresses the hazard of surface fault rupture"; the Seismic Hazards Mapping Act covers liquefaction, landslides, amplified shaking, tsunami/seiche; sellers must deliver a Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement (Civil Code 1103).
**Not verified:** whether the subject parcel is inside an A-P zone or a seismic hazard zone (requires parcel lookup). Recorded as U-02.

## Source N — USGS ShakeOut Scenario
URL: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/shakeout-scenario-a-hypothetical-mw78-earthquake-southern-san-andreas-fault
Verified: hypothetical **M7.8** on the southern San Andreas Fault; modelled "to cause about **1,800 deaths and $213 billion of economic losses**" (USGS OF 2008-1150 overview page, https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1150/).
Interpretation limit: this is a *scenario*, not a forecast of the coming event and not a loss estimate for any individual parcel.
