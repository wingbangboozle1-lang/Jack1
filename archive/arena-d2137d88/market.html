NNN / QSR market, tenant credit & sale friction

There is no official government cap-rate index. Every cap-rate or comp on this page is broker/market data, badged market/amber and repeated on Flagged for Review. Only lease-law and tax figures (Measure ULA, Prop 13, base transfer tax) are official-source.

Market-data disclaimer. Cap rates move weekly and vary by term, escalation, guarantee and location. These rows establish an order of magnitude (≈4.3% stale Westwood ask; ≈4.91% TTM Mar-2024; ≈5.0–6.5% new 20-yr offerings, Dec 2025) — not a valuation. Get a broker BOV and, if a transaction is contemplated, an MAI appraisal.

Measure ULA — the single biggest sale-decision friction

  • What it is. LA City “Homelessness and Housing Solutions Tax” (approved Nov 2022, effective 2023-04-01): an extra documentary transfer tax on LA City real-property sales (residential AND commercial) on top of the base 0.45% city + 0.11% county tax.
  • Rates (2026, Chained-CPI-adjusted). 4.0% of consideration above ≈$5.4M; 5.5% at ≈$10.9M and above — per the LA City Administrative Officer’s November 2026 report. Thresholds ratchet upward each April with Chained CPI. Verify the live figure at finance.lacity.gov before any LOI.
  • Who pays. Customarily the seller. On a $6.5M sale: ≈4.0% ULA ≈ $260K + ≈0.56% base ≈ $36.4K ≈ $296K of friction before capital gains/depreciation recapture — enough to outweigh a year or two of rent growth.
  • What does NOT avoid it. A 1031 exchange defers federal/state capital gains — it does not exempt ULA (ULA attaches to the LA City conveyance itself). Seller-financing games, fractional-interest splits and entity transfers are aggressive/untested or explicitly policed; do not structure around ULA without counsel.
  • Practical consequences. (a) If the asset is near the $5.4M threshold, pricing strategy matters; (b) selling into unincorporated LA County / Glendale / Burbank avoids ULA for the next owner — shifting relative demand and supporting LA City NNN pricing discipline; (c) holding avoids triggering ULA until exit — but holding an unrenewed NNN has its own decay (see Decision).
  • Buyer behavior. Market reporting (flagged secondary): some buyer demand has shifted to LA-adjacent cities to avoid ULA on resale; institutional buyers still transact but underwrite ULA into pricing.

Tenant credit — read this twice

  • JACK is not S&P/Moody's rated (broker-reported, Mar 2024, stale — flagged). Financials: FY2025 revenue ≈$1.5B, adj. EBITDA ≈$270.9M, same-store sales −4.2%, dividend discontinued, ~$115M Del Taco divestiture, activist-contested 2026 annual meeting, “JACK on Track” turnaround. FY2024/FY2025 net losses per aggregator (−$36.7M / −$80.7M, flagged secondary).
  • 93% of restaurants are franchised (1,986 of 2,136, FY2025 10-K). The cash-flow covenant at your store is most likely a franchisee entity, frequently an LLC with limited standalone assets, often with a personal guarantee of unknown quality. JACK parent guarantees are the exception, not the rule.
  • Implications for NNN value: (1) buyers price the weakest link — franchisee P&L, not JACK consolidated; (2) at renewal, franchisees will resist big escalations (they face $20/hr labor + food inflation); (3) in a stress case, the landlord’s remedy is dark-store risk + re-tenanting a highly specific 2,000 SF QSR box — lease re-use is easy physically but the brand covenant is gone; (4) JACK’s own store-closure program (closures outnumbered new units in FY2025) signals system shrinkage — unprofitable stores get shuttered, and your asset could be one.
  • What kills NNN value fastest: a franchisee default + unrenewed expiry in a rising-cap-rate tape. Everything else is negotiable; that combination is not.
