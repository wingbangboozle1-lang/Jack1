3 · The market in one paragraph

Central LA sits inside the second-largest metro economy in the U.S. Population is flat-to-declining: LA city 3.869M (−0.8% vs 2020) and LA County 9.695M (−3.2% vs 2020) as of July 1, 2025 — a slow but real headwind for local QSR traffic. Incomes are solid: LA County median household income $90,112 (2020–24) vs LA city $81,939, with 16.5% city-level poverty — the value-QSR segment is structurally favored. Employment is soft: 5.0% county unemployment (Dec 2025, NSA) vs 5.7% annual 2025 and 5.1% in 2023; LAEDC projects ~1.3% real GCP growth in 2026 with payrolls +0.2%. Trade anchors the economy: Port of LA moved 10.24M TEUs in 2025 (26th consecutive year as the nation’s busiest). 2028 Olympics investment is a genuine but uneven catalyst. Schools (LAUSD graduation 86.5%, 2025; enrollment 497,105, falling) signal a stable-but-aging trade area rather than a growth corridor. Market detail → · Economy detail →

6 · Recommendation

Market-test a sale now (2026–2027), ideally via a 1031 exchange into a stronger-credit net-lease replacement or diversified portfolio. Do not hold passively toward expiry. If — and only if — the tenant executes a ≥10-year renewal with ≥2% annual escalations, an unconditional parent (or investment-grade franchisee) guarantee, and store-level profitability evidence, before ~2028, then renewing becomes the better risk-adjusted hold. Every other path favors selling.

Risk-balance rationale: (1) Asymmetry — upside of holding for 4–9 more years is capped by escalation clauses and flat population, while downside includes tenant distress, dark-store re-leasing, and cap-rate expansion; (2) Option value decays — every year of WALT lost re-prices the asset against the Owner; (3) Timing — net-lease retail cap rates have stabilized (6.55% Q4 2025) and the tenant is itself selling real estate into this window; (4) Black swans — Puente Hills M7.0–7.5 scenario (USGS/SCEC: 3,000–18,000 deaths; ~$250B loss) sits directly beneath the site; insurance availability in California is tightening post-2025 wildfires ($4.6–8.9B LA County economic hit per LAEDC); holding concentrates this tail risk, selling transfers it at full price.
