Read the break-even honestly
At the illustrative defaults, the hold path beats selling whenever renewal probability exceeds ≈23%. The case for selling therefore does not rest on this deterministic NPV — it rests on (i) renewal with acceptable terms (≥10 yrs, ≥2% bumps, real guarantee) being unlikely to materialize in writing by ~2028 from a 6.3x-leverage tenant mid-closure-program; (ii) unpriced tail risks (casualty — Puente Hills; insurance failure; closure selection; WALT-cliff bifurcation) that are borne every extra year of hold and transfer at full price on sale; and (iii) the option structure: market-testing the sale costs nothing and buys information — the tenant must then either sign the extension or lose the asset. Anyone claiming a single-number answer without your lease data is guessing.

B = PV(rents 2026→expiry) + value_renew ÷ (1+d)^years; C same with re-lease net proceeds; Weighted = PV + [p·value_renew + (1−p)·(value_release − vacancy loss − re-tenant cost)] ÷ (1+d)^years. Renewal branch assumes 10-year hold at base cap with reset rent. Sell-now is not discounted.

Inputs — illustrative ⚑ (replace)

  • Base rent/NOI, escalation, expiry year — not provided; examples only

  • WALT penalty schedule — modeling convention, not market data
  • Renewal probability 60% — subjective; driven by tenant distress (6.3x leverage). A bear case (40%) or bull case (80%) moves the weighted NPV by ±~$0.1–0.3M at these scales

  • Renewal branch assumes 10-year hold at base cap with reset rent — simplification, not a commitment
Honesty rules applied
(1) Every default is either sourced or flagged ⚑ illustrative. (2) No property-specific number was invented. (3) Outputs are scenario arithmetic, reproducible by hand: Value = NOI/Cap; NPV = Σ rent_t/(1+d)^t + terminal/(1+d)^n. (4) The model never recommends leverage; it informs the sell/renew asymmetry. (5) Re-run quarterly with the lease abstract and a live cap-rate quote from a licensed CA broker.
