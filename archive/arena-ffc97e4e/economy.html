Why this matters for the lease question
The Jan 2025 fires were a ~$5–9B local-GDP event concentrated in residential zones; a Central LA QSR was not burned, but the second-order effects — construction-cost inflation, insurance re-pricing and availability, FAIR Plan crowding, displaced households — directly raise operating costs and re-sale risk premiums for every LA asset. Insurance and earthquake coverage are now first-order diligence items, not boilerplate.

Research · Economy

Economic activity — Los Angeles County & MSA

Scale, growth, and stress points for the central-LA economy. Official figures (BEA, BLS, Census) are badged official; DTLA office distress appears only as clearly-flagged secondary press/association estimates because no official vacancy series exists.

Reading the economy for this asset

  • Scale without momentum. A $1.003T current-dollar county economy (2nd-largest U.S. county economy per secondary press reading of BEA 2024 tables — flagged) growing in real terms only +2.1% in 2024. LA is not shrinking, but it is not booming; NNN underwriting should use flat real growth.
  • Labor market is the pivot for QSR traffic. 5.3% 2025 average MSA unemployment (4.8% Dec 2025) is soft but improving from 2023–2024; labor force ≈6.73M. A return to ≤4.5% would support rent growth; a slide back to ≥6% would pressure the franchisee and any renewal terms.
  • Sector mix favors central LA's service economy. Information ($134B), professional/business services ($120.3B) and real estate ($105.9B) dominate county GDP (USAFacts/BEA — flagged secondary). These sectors feed hybrid/remote work patterns that hollow out 5-day downtown lunch traffic — the structural headwind cited for JIB daypart sales.
  • Catalyst stack 2026–2028. D Line Section 1 opened 2026-05-08 (3 stations; full buildout to Westwood/VA forecast 2026–2027, ≈$9.7B per secondary press), LA28 Olympics July 2028, and continued DTLA residential absorption (≈91% multifamily occupancy) all support 2027–2029 central-LA foot traffic. These are real, dated, verifiable events — but they are upside, not base case.
  • Restaurant economics are regulated. The $20/hr California fast-food minimum (2024-04-01, DIR) compresses franchisee margins and makes large rent escalators at renewal hard to absorb — expect operators to resist big bumps and lenders to haircut franchisee guarantees.
  • Homelessness trend is improving but material. City of LA count 43,695 (LAHSA, Jan 2025, second annual decline) — central-LA QSR operators still absorb security/loitering costs; this is a real operating risk and a tenant-relations lever in renewal talks.
Watch items (quarterly): BLS LA MSA unemployment release; JACK next 10-Qs (same-store sales, liquidity, franchisee health, any renewed guidance on dividend/store closures); DTLA residential leasing; LA28 venue build-out; any Measure ULA litigation/amendment; 10Y Treasury path.
