Area projection · scenario bands · decision triggers

Project the area, then test the lease against it.

No parcel, lease, or rent was supplied, so this page cannot project this property. What it can do is project the area from published official series, show the uncertainty band those agencies publish themselves, and state the thresholds at which the sale-versus-renew balance should change.

What this projection system will and will not produce

Four rules govern every number below. 1. Only published official series are used: the SCAG adopted forecast, BLS local-area unemployment, BEA output, and Census estimates. 2. Every calculated value is labelled derived and shows the published inputs it came from, so it can be re-done by hand. 3. The uncertainty band is the one the forecasting agency publishes for its own low and high scenarios — it is not invented here. 4. No property value, rent, cap rate, or probability of a loss is produced. Those cells stay blank until property documents arrive.

Three area scenarios

Slow, baseline, faster — and what each would change

These are the forecasting agency’s own scenarios, restated in decision terms. They describe the area, never this unit’s sales, rent, or resale price.

Slow · SCAG low case

Published band: 19.45 million regionally in 2030 and 19.6 in 2035.

What would have to be true: housing supply and affordability keep suppressing in-migration, so the region cannot fill its own job demand — the mechanism SCAG describes for its low scenario.

Decision implication: weaker rent growth and a thinner buyer pool at expiry argue for converting the asset earlier rather than later; the lease term becomes the main thing being paid for.

What would falsify it: county unemployment holding below its long-run median while permits and population rise.

SCAG low scenario

Baseline · SCAG point forecast

Published band: 19.7 million in 2030, 20.1 in 2035, 20.8 in 2050; regional jobs 8.937 million (2022) to 10.276 million (2050).

What would have to be true: births, deaths, migration, participation, household formation and employment evolve near the expert panel’s central assumptions.

Decision implication: a slow, steady area does not by itself justify either an immediate sale or an unconditional renewal; the lease economics and the tenant’s credit decide it.

What would falsify it: a sustained move of county unemployment well above the historical median, or a published output contraction of the 2020 magnitude.

SCAG point forecast

Faster · SCAG high case

Published band: 19.95 million in 2030 and 20.5 in 2035.

What would have to be true: childcare, healthcare, technology and infrastructure investment lift participation and migration, as SCAG’s high scenario assumes.

Decision implication: holding with a signed longer term captures more of the upside, and a sale now would transfer that growth to the buyer.

What would falsify it: population and employment revisions that keep undershooting the adopted forecast.

SCAG high scenario
