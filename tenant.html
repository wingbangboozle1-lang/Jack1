Tenant credit

The logo is not the obligor.

A Jack in the Box sign over the door tells you the brand, not who owes the rent. The company's own 10-K states that for a majority of franchised sites it leases or subleases the real property to the franchisee. So the landlord's counterparty could be the brand, a franchisee, or a brand affiliate that subleases. Which one it is here is the largest unverified input in this brief — and it changes the credit quality of the rent more than any market statistic on this site.

Credit assessment in one paragraph

What the filings add up to

Arguments that the rent is safe
  • A national brand with 2,136 restaurants across 21 states and a 75-year operating history.
  • The prototype box is small and cheap to operate, and the drive-thru entitlement is scarce in built-out Los Angeles.
  • Rent is typically a modest share of restaurant sales for quick-service units, so a unit can stay profitable through a sales decline.
  • Guide-level Adjusted EBITDA of $225–240m for FY2026 is a solvent enterprise.
Arguments that the rent is at risk
  • Same-store sales deteriorating into the fourth quarter of FY2025 (−7.4%).
  • Net closures of 55 units in one year, with 50–100 more guided.
  • The dividend was discontinued — cash is being conserved.
  • Management states its plan includes selling real estate: the counterparty is monetising property, not adding it.
  • An active proxy contest with an activist shareholder, disclosed in the 10-K.
  • ~70% of the system sits in California and Texas, which the company itself flags as a concentration.

Balance: the enterprise is solvent; the site is not guaranteed. A brand can be healthy and still close a specific underperforming unit — and a closure list of 50–100 sites a year is exactly the mechanism by which that happens. Underwriting the site means underwriting the operator's unit-level economics, which are not public.

Franchise terms

Why a 20-year franchise term is not a 20-year lease

The company's standard franchise agreement runs 20 years with a $50,000 initial fee and a 5.0% royalty. A landlord reading "20-year term" could wrongly assume a 20-year rent obligation. The franchise agreement is between the brand and the operator. The lease is a separate contract with separate dates, and its term may be shorter, longer, or renewed on different cycles. The owner's stated 2030–2035 expiry window is the lease, not the franchise agreement.
