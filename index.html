Executive summary · prepared 6 November 2026 (UTC)

One Jack in the Box, one NNN lease, one decision:
hold, renew, or exit before 2030–2035.

This dashboard answers a single question with public, official data only: is it better to sell this central-Los Angeles net-lease restaurant before the lease expires, or to renew it? 130 data rows, 91 of them verified line by line against a government or SEC source. Where a value could not be confirmed it is left blank and published openly on the Flagged for Review page.

Recommendation

Do not sell into a short lease, and do not wait for expiry. Lock the renewal now, then sell the term.

The asset's value is its remaining lease term, not its building. Two things destroy that value: a tenant that walks, and a buyer market that will not finance a lease with fewer than five years left. Both are approaching. The sequence that preserves the most wealth under the widest set of assumptions is:

  1. 2027 — pull the lease, estoppel and guarantor file; commission an appraisal and a broker opinion of value.
  2. 2027–2028 — open renewal talks with the tenant for a 10+ year term at a market reset, with a corporate guaranty if the operator is a franchisee. Renewal is the value event, not the fallback.
  3. 2028–2031 — if a market renewal is signed, sell into a long remaining term at the tighter cap rate. If the tenant will not sign, market the property immediately, while at least three years of term remain.

Why not hold to expiry? The model clears holding only if the tenant renews with probability above 59.4%. A tenant that just posted −4.2% full-year and −7.4% fourth-quarter same-store sales, closed 86 units, guided 50–100 more closures, cancelled its dividend and is selling real estate does not earn a coin-flip-plus assumption. The six findings that drive the decision

1 · The tenant is shrinking, not growing

2,191 units (FY2024) → 2,136 (FY2025) → guided 2,050–2,100 for FY2026, with 50–100 closures guided and "selling real estate" written into the guidance itself. Same-store sales are negative at every level and the dividend has been eliminated.

Tenant & lease evidence →

2 · The catchment is not growing either

Los Angeles County lost 3.2% of its population between 2020 and 2025 and the state's official projection has it falling another 1.5% by 2035. Payroll growth is +0.5% year on year; Information is down 5.7%. Rent growth cannot be underwritten from volume.

Market evidence →

3 · The exit is taxed, and the tax is unavoidable-by-timing

A City of Los Angeles sale above $5m carries a 4.0% Measure ULA transfer tax (5.5% above $10m) paid by the seller, on top of documentary transfer taxes and advisory costs. That is roughly 6% of price consumed at exit, whichever year you choose — so the question is not whether to pay it, but whether price and term justify it this year.

Risk register →

4 · Money is expensive, so buyers pay for term

With the 10-year at 4.90%, a leveraged buyer of a single-tenant asset needs a long lease to get financing. The buyer pool is therefore a function of the calendar: every year the lease runs down, the pool shrinks. That is a wasting asset with a deadline, and the deadline is written into the lease.

Projection model →

5 · The tail risks are regional, not parcel-level — and uninsured

An M7.8 southern San Andreas event is modelled at ~1,800 deaths and $213bn of regional losses. The January 2025 Palisades Fire destroyed 6,845 structures. Neither is a forecast; both are the reason the FAIR Plan exists and why commercial insurance is the least negotiable line item in a NNN budget.

Catastrophe exposure →

6 · What we do not know is bigger than what we do

28 rows on this site are deliberately blank: the lease itself, the parcel number, the rent, the renewal options, the insurance policy and the parcel-level hazard designations. None of them can be filled from a public source. Every one of them can change the answer, so they are published, not papered over.

How to read this site

Every row in every table ends with a link to the official page it came from. Green = verified on that page. Amber = simple arithmetic on two verified values. Red = could not be confirmed, so the value is blank. Purple = private property data that is withheld on purpose. Nothing on this site is estimated, interpolated or "filled in from experience".

Privacy & scope statement

No personal information appears anywhere in this product. No owner name, no street address, no assessor parcel number, no rent figure, no lender, no tenant entity and no contact detail is published. Those fields exist in the data as explicit placeholders marked placeholder. Research notes kept in the repository are aggregate public statistics only; they contain no personal data to protect.

Geographic scope: the analysis uses Los Angeles city and Los Angeles County as the official statistical frame, because no parcel-level identifier was supplied. A "central Los Angeles" submarket is therefore treated as a qualitative overlay, never as a fabricated statistic. The property-specific conclusions are conditional on that limitation and are stated as such.

This is an analytical work product for the owner's internal decision-making. It is not an appraisal, not investment advice, and not a substitute for a licensed appraisal, a title report, a lease abstract, or tax counsel.
