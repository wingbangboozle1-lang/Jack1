Jack in the Box · reported NNN lease · central Los Angeles (approximate)

Hold the asset, but negotiate the term now — and pre-commit the exit.

Official data cannot price this parcel, because the parcel, the lease and the counterparty are private. What the official data can do is rule out two of the four available paths and rank the remainder. The county is losing population on the state's own baseline, the tenant is closing restaurants and selling real estate, and rates are high. That combination argues against an open-ended hold and against an opportunistic sale — and for converting an expiring lease into a longer one while simultaneously preparing a sale file.

Recommended path: renew, then decide. Not because the area is strong, but because the lease term is the only asset the owner fully controls, and it is worth more than the price a buyer would pay for a lease with five years left on it.

Executive summary

What the evidence actually says

  1. The area is shrinking, not growing. Los Angeles County lost 3.2% of its population between 2020 and 2025 and the Department of Finance projects a further 1.47% decline to 2035, while California as a whole grows 2.23% over the same period. A volume-driven restaurant in a shrinking county is fighting its own catchment. Population rows →
  2. The tenant is contracting. System same-store sales fell 4.2% in FY2025 and 7.4% in the fourth quarter. It closed 86 restaurants against 31 openings, guides another 50–100 closures, discontinued its dividend, and states that its turnaround plan includes selling real estate. A renewal is therefore a negotiation with a counterparty that has alternatives. Tenant rows →
  3. The jobs picture is flat, with one growing sector. Metro nonfarm employment is up 0.5% year over year. Information is down 5.7%, financial activities 1.7%, professional services 0.5%; education and health services is up 3.8% and leisure and hospitality 1.5%. The durable daypart base is health and education, not office workers. Jobs rows →
  4. The cost picture is hostile to the customer. Gasoline is up 30.8% year over year and energy 20.3%, while restaurant menu prices are up 2.8%. A drive-thru customer paying a third more at the pump and a restaurant raising prices below headline inflation is a margin squeeze in both directions. Price rows →
  5. The sell case is real, but smaller than the headline price. A city transfer tax of 4.45% (or 5.95% above $10.9m) plus commission plus income tax on the gain plus loan payoff is a large deduction from gross proceeds. An owner who compares a gross price to a hold value will overstate the sale. Net-proceeds section →
  6. Hazards are diligence items, not probabilities. This brief assigns no probability to wildfire, earthquake or flood for the parcel. It does record what a realised event looks like: the Palisades Fire destroyed 6,845 structures; the ShakeOut scenario models $191bn of regional loss. The material channel for a central-LA box is insurance-market contagion, not ember exposure. Hazard rows →

Recommended course of action

Renew, then decide — with a written trigger

Ask the tenant for a 10-year extension with a creditworthy guaranty and a rent no lower than the current rent, in exchange for a defined capital contribution (roof, HVAC, drive-thru lane). In parallel, assemble the sale file: appraisal, rent roll, lease abstract, insurance quote, and a §1031 replacement search.

Do not sign a long extension at a weak rent with no guaranty. Do not hold to expiry with no re-use plan. Do not sell today at a price struck off a declining same-store-sales trend unless liquidity is needed.

Why "it depends" is the honest answer on price No official source publishes a cap rate, an appraisal or a comparable sale for an individual single-tenant restaurant. The 33 blank rows on this site are not gaps in the research — they are fields that cannot be sourced officially and therefore must not be filled in (full list).
What would change the recommendation Three findings would move this answer: (1) the lease is guaranteed by Jack in the Box Inc. — that raises the value of holding and of a renewal; (2) the tenant already holds renewal options below market — that destroys the landlord's leverage and pushes toward an early sale; (3) the owner needs liquidity within 36 months — that overrides the asset analysis entirely.

Five steps, in order

What to do in the next 90 days

Full path comparison →
  1. Confirm the counterparty before anything else. The lease may be with Jack in the Box Inc., with a franchisee, or with a brand affiliate that subleases to a franchisee. The 10-K says the company leases or subleases to franchisees for most franchised sites. Unverified See PR-02 and F-11.
  2. Read the lease for the four clauses that decide this case. Renewal options, rent escalations, the casualty and rent-abatement clause, and the assignment and guaranty language. Until those are read, every rent comparison is speculative. See PR-09, PR-10.
  3. Get two numbers, not one. A broker opinion of value for the asset as it stands today, and an after-tax net-proceeds figure. The city transfer tax alone is 4.45% of price in the $5.4m–$10.9m band, before income tax on the gain.
  4. Open the renewal conversation early and price the alternative. Renewal leverage decays as expiry approaches. Approach the tenant 24–30 months out, with a re-use or redevelopment plan in hand so that a weak offer can be declined.
  5. Pre-commit a sell trigger now, in writing. Example: sell if the tenant will not extend beyond 2030 on acceptable terms by a stated date, or if the parcel's insurance quote exceeds a stated level. A pre-committed trigger prevents a decision made under pressure.
