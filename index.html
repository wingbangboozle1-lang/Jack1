Executive summary

What the evidence supports

  1. Do not treat “Central Los Angeles” as one market. The Census, SCAG regional forecast, and Downtown Community Plan use different geographies. None can be assigned to this restaurant without a parcel location. See scope and indicators.
  2. Long-run regional growth is positive in the official baseline, but not a site forecast. SCAG’s adopted 2024 plan projects Los Angeles County population and employment through 2050. Downtown planning expectations apply only inside the Downtown Plan boundary and are not guaranteed outcomes. Read the projection table.
  3. The brand does not identify the lease guarantor. Jack in the Box’s latest reviewed quarterly filing reports both company-operated and franchise-operated restaurants. The specific landlord counterparty and any parent guarantee remain unknown. See tenant context.
  4. Tail hazards are material to the decision, and only part of the county-scale evidence survives an official read. USGS/UCERF3 states a 60% probability of M ≥ 6.7 (46% for M ≥ 7.0, 31% for M ≥ 7.5) within 30 years in the Los Angeles region, re-read on the USGS page on 08 Nov 2026. FEMA's OpenFEMA declaration summaries count 88 federal disaster designations for Los Angeles County since 1969 — 61 fire, 10 flood, 8 severe storm — counted directly through the federal API on 08 Nov 2026. County National Risk Index scores and expected annual loss are still withheld: they live inside FEMA's interactive tools and are not served by OpenFEMA. The reported 26% expansion of CAL FIRE Very High zones still could not be located on a state page. All of this is county-scale screening, not a parcel finding. Open the risk register.
  5. The regional economy is large and diverse, but parcel-level demand is unverified. LA metro GDP is $1.295 trillion (2023 — the latest year published on a series the source now marks discontinued) and LA County GDP is $1.003 trillion (2024). City population is down 0.8% since 2020. County unemployment averaged 5.6% across the eleven published months of 2025 and stood at 4.5% in September 2026 (preliminary) — an improving rate on a labour force that is itself 1.9% smaller than in December 2025. A $1.355 trillion "2024" metro figure that appeared in an earlier draft could not be confirmed anywhere official and is disclosed on Flagged for Review rather than published. These are macro conditions, not evidence of demand at this site. See area indicators · See the projection system.

Recommended course of action
Do not sell at random and do not wait for expiry. Confirm the contract, get a market price while term remains, and ask the tenant for an extension now: the value of a single-tenant NNN asset is carried by remaining lease term, and the one unrecoverable outcome is arriving at 2030–2035 with no renewal and no sale process behind you.

Course of action

The sequence to run, in order

Each step is something the owner controls before the lease clock forces a decision. Steps 1–3 can run in parallel and cost relatively little; step 4 is the only step that produces the actual sell-versus-renew number.

Ranked recommendation

What to do now

  1. Confirm the contract. Obtain the executed lease and every amendment, estoppel, and guaranty; calendar all notice, exercise, and lender-consent dates; establish whether 2030–2035 is a fixed expiry, a series of options, or both. Lease fields still open.
  2. Get a price while term remains. Commission an independent valuation and solicit two or three unsolicited indicative bids. With roughly four to nine years of reported term left, the asset is still financeable for a net-lease buyer; that changes as term shortens. See the term-and-cap-rate arithmetic.
  3. Ask the tenant for an early extension. Target at least five added years with fixed or CPI-linked escalators, clear expense allocation, and a defined assignment and guaranty position. Ask for the unit's sales and occupancy-cost figures as part of the conversation: renewal capacity is a unit-economics question, not a brand question. Tenant context.
  4. Compare after-tax net proceeds with risk-adjusted hold value. Deduct payoff, closing costs, transfer tax (0.45% base, plus 4.0%/5.5% Measure ULA above $5.4M/$10.9M for closings after 30 June 2026), and owner-level tax; discount hold cash flow at a rate anchored to the 4.84% 10-year Treasury plus a property-risk spread. Worksheet and formulas.
  5. Decide on the comparison, not on the calendar. If the extension is signed at acceptable rent, hold and re-test annually. If it is refused, or if the property's risk profile is unacceptable, sell while at least three years of term still exist rather than arriving at expiry unrenewed.

Why this order and not "sell now" or "hold to expiry"

Selling now forfeits the option value of a signed extension and pays 4.45%–5.95% in City transfer tax on a large consideration if ULA applies, before any owner-level tax.

Holding to expiry converts a leased income asset into a vacant building whose value depends on a re-tenanting market the owner has not underwritten — with no income during downtime and full exposure to hazard and insurance conditions in the interim.

Running all three tracks in parallel costs a valuation and some broker and legal time, and keeps both the sale option and the renewal option alive until the lease itself resolves the question.
