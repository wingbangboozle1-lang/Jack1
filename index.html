Bottom-line answer

What the evidence says about selling vs. renewing — balanced for wealth preservation and growth at manageable risk.

Recommended • Base Case

Prepare to sell in the 2028–2031 window — but keep renewal as a hedge

Central LA offers resilient demand (dense, transit-rich, high foot traffic) yet faces structural county population decline (-1.3% to 2030 per CA DOF) and Very High natural-hazard exposure ($7.6B expected annual loss, FEMA). Combined with a non-investment-grade tenant (B+) and a 20-year NNN roll, the best risk-adjusted path is market the asset 18–30 months before expiry while simultaneously negotiating a renewal option. This preserves optionality and captures QSR investor demand before re-leasing risk peaks.

Risk score: Medium-High if held past 2032 without renewal
When to renew instead

Renew if you secure rent-to-market + protections

Renewal dominates if you can lock: (1) 10-year+ extension with 5–10% rent bumps every 5 years, (2) corporate-level guarantee (not franchisee-only), (3) tenant-funded refresh and clear seismic/wildfire resilience upgrades, and (4) insurance pass-throughs that keep NOI intact through hazard repricing. Central LA’s density and Metro investment support stable QSR sales in the interim.

  • LA City median HH income $81,939 — above U.S. median
  • LA County unemployment 5.0–5.9% (2024–25), trending stable
  • Metro +53% ridership recovery in 4 years; K Line +139% weekdays
What would change the answer

Watch these triggers

  • Population surprise: LA City regained 31,276 residents in 2024 — if gains persist 2–3 years, renewal leverage improves.
  • Cap-rate shift: QSR avg 5.71% (Q2 2025). A 50–75 bps compression favors selling; expansion favors holding for yield.
  • Hazard repricing: CAL FIRE 2025 expanded Very High zones 29% — insurance cost spike erodes NNN appeal.
  • Seismic event: USGS 60% chance of M≥6.7 in next 30 years — unreinforced structures lose value fastest.

All triggers are tracked in Risks → with official sources.

✓
Wealth preservation principle: In a Very High-hazard, high-cost, slowly shrinking county, you preserve wealth by monetizing optionality early (selling with term left) or by converting uncertainty into contract (long renewal with escalations and guarantees). Holding to expiry with no renewal is the highest-risk path.

Why this question is hard — and how we make it manageable

A NNN asset looks passive until lease expiry concentrates all risks at once. Central LA amplifies both upside and downside.

The “bond-like” illusion

A triple-net lease feels like a bond: tenant pays rent, taxes, insurance, maintenance (NNN). But unlike a bond, at expiry in 2030–2035 you face binary re-leasing risk — if the tenant leaves, you own a single-tenant QSR box on a parcel that may need seismic, fire, and ADA upgrades to re-tenant.

Jack in the Box Inc. is not investment-grade (S&P/Moody’s: N/A; market-implied B+ per NNN research). 90%+ of its ~2,200 units are franchised (SEC 10-K Nov 21, 2024). Your guarantee may be a franchisee, not the parent — a material credit distinction we flag as Needs Review on Real Estate →.

Central LA: dense ≠ growing

With 8,304 people/mi² (Census), Central LA is among the densest U.S. urban cores. Density supports QSR visitation even as the county shrinks. That is why Koreatown/Wilshire rents ($1,762–$1,933) stay high despite county out-migration — housing shortage + transit access mute population decline at the neighborhood scale.

What moves value between now and 2035

  • Rent vs. market: Does current rent sit below/above today’s QSR market ($129,846 NOI prototype, $1,083/SF sale comps — flagged as market-sourced, not official)?
  • Cap rates: QSR avg 5.71% (Q2 2025, +2 bps) vs 6.08% all STNL — 37 bps premium for QSR. If rates fall, your building cap compresses and selling is attractive; if they rise, holding for yield wins. (Market source → flagged.)
  • Insurance & hazard repricing: CAL FIRE 2025 expanded Very High zones 29%; FEMA $7.6B EAL — NNN does not immunize you if tenant can’t afford NNN passthroughs after a re-rating.
  • Transit upside: Downtown Community Plan adds capacity for 872k new residents in LA City by 2045 (SCAG) — more housing near your site supports QSR sales.
