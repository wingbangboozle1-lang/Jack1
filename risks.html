Risk register

16 risks, ranked by whether the owner can act on them.

Two of these risks — the counterparty's identity and the lease's terms — dominate everything else on this page. A catastrophe model on a parcel whose lease you have not read is decoration. The register is therefore ordered by controllability, not by drama: what the owner can negotiate, then what the market will do to them, then the tail that nobody can price.

The honest limitation on this register No probability is attached to any black-swan event for this parcel. Parcel-level hazard data requires an address that is withheld, and no official source publishes a per-parcel event probability. The register describes how each event would reach the owner's cash flow and what to check, which is actionable. Inventing a probability would not be.

Detail

Trigger, transmission and mitigation

R-01 Business as usual

Tenant walks at expiry instead of renewing

Trigger evidence
Guided 50-100 closures in FY2026 (T-11); FY2025 same-store sales -4.2% (T-06) and -7.4% in Q4 (T-07).
How it reaches the owner
A dark box on a drive-thru parcel reverts to landlord: property tax, insurance, security, and a re-letting period. The prototype is only 1,372 sq ft (T-20), so the building is not the value - the land and the conditional-use entitlement for a drive-thru are.
Mitigation
Start renewal dialogue 24-30 months before expiry, not 6. Confirm in writing whether the tenant holds options (PR-10). Have a re-use plan for the parcel: another QSR, a conversion, or redevelopment.
Residual exposure
Medium probability; high severity if no fallback use exists

So what: This is the highest-probability adverse outcome and the one the owner can actually influence. It is a negotiation and timing risk, not a market risk.

R-02 Business as usual

Renewal is agreed but at a lower rent than today

Trigger evidence
Brand-level sales decline (T-06, T-07); the tenant's stated plan includes selling real estate (T-25).
How it reaches the owner
The landlord keeps occupancy and loses income. In a flat-to-declining population county (J-05, J-06) the tenant has credible alternatives, so the negotiating asymmetry favours the tenant.
Mitigation
Model a renewal at a 0 to -15% rent reset and check the after-tax hold value still beats the sale. Ask for term length and credit (guaranty) rather than headline rent.
Residual exposure
Medium probability; medium severity

So what: A renewal is not automatically a win. A long renewal at a weak rent with no guaranty may be worse than a sale to a 1031 buyer who values the remaining term.

R-03 Business as usual

The counterparty is the franchisee, not the brand

Trigger evidence
The 10-K states the company leases or subleases to the franchisee for a majority of franchised sites (T-04).
How it reaches the owner
Landlord credit is the operator's balance sheet, not a national brand's. A single-unit or small multi-unit franchisee can fail while the brand is healthy, and the brand is not obliged to keep the site open.
Mitigation
Read the actual lease and any guaranty before any decision (PR-03, T-27). If there is no brand guaranty, underwrite the operator as an independent small business.
Residual exposure
Unknown probability - must be resolved first

So what: This single unknown changes the risk class of the whole asset, which is why it is flagged as F-11.

R-04 Business as usual

Sale proceeds are eroded by transfer tax and tax on gain

Trigger evidence
Measure ULA 4.0%/5.5% plus a 0.45% city base tax (X-01 to X-07); federal and state tax on gain depends on basis (PR-22, X-13).
How it reaches the owner
Net proceeds, not gross price, is what the owner can redeploy. On a $6.5m sale the city-level transfer tax alone is 4.45% - and the thresholds are CPI-indexed every year, so the arithmetic changes.
Mitigation
Compute the after-tax, after-cost number before any letter of intent. If the intent is to stay invested in real estate, test a §1031 exchange; if not, the tax is simply a cost of exiting.
Residual exposure
High probability of occurring if a sale happens; severity set by basis

So what: An owner who compares a sale price to a hold value without deducting 4.45%-5.95% plus income tax will overstate the sell case by a wide margin.

R-05 Business as usual

Insurance cost or availability deteriorates

Trigger evidence
The Department of Insurance describes insurers withdrawing from areas as FAIR Plan enrolment grows (Z-13).
How it reaches the owner
Under a true NNN lease the tenant pays premiums, so the landlord is insulated while the lease runs. After expiry the landlord carries it - and an uninsurable parcel is a hard stop for most buyers and lenders.
Mitigation
Obtain a current quote now, before deciding. Ask the tenant for the existing policy declarations. Confirm whether the lease obliges the tenant to insure and what happens if it cannot.
Residual exposure
Medium probability; high severity if the parcel is in a mapped hazard zone

So what: Insurance is the quietest way a central-LA commercial parcel loses value: not through damage, but through the loss of a market.

R-06 Business as usual

Interest rates stay high or rise, compressing the sale multiple

Trigger evidence
10-year Treasury at 4.84% (C-01), up from the 2020-2021 range; no official cap-rate series exists for this asset (C-03).
How it reaches the owner
A buyer's required return moves with the risk-free rate. Every 100 bp of cap-rate expansion on a fixed rent stream is roughly a 15-20% value change at typical net-lease spreads.
Mitigation
Rate the decision on cash flow and optionality rather than on a hoped-for multiple. If a sale is the goal, do not wait for a rate environment that may not return.
Residual exposure
Medium probability; medium-to-high severity

So what: Higher-for-longer is the base case the official data supports. A hold decision should not depend on cap-rate compression.

R-07 Business as usual

Population and household decline in the county

Trigger evidence
County population -3.2% since 2020 (D-04); DOF projects a further -1.47% to 2035 (J-06); the state grows +2.23% over the same period (J-09).
How it reaches the owner
Fewer residents is fewer transactions at the counter. Restaurants absorb this through price and hours before they close, so it reaches the landlord as weaker renewal terms rather than immediately as vacancy.
Mitigation
Weight the decision toward the durability of the location and the parcel's alternative use rather than toward county population growth, which is negative on the official baseline.
Residual exposure
High probability of continuation; medium severity

So what: This is the clearest official signal in the dataset that the area is not a growth market for a volume-driven restaurant.

R-08 Business as usual

Operating-cost regulation squeezes the operator's margin

Trigger evidence
California fast-food minimum wage $20.00/hour since April 2024 (X-11), with a statutory council able to raise it; local minimum wages on top.
How it reaches the owner
The operator's rent-paying capacity is a residual after labour. A wage floor that rises faster than menu prices (menu inflation 2.8%, P-04) compresses that residual.
Mitigation
Underwrite rent-to-sales at the operator level, not the brand level. Check which city minimum wage applies (X-12, unverified).
Residual exposure
Medium probability; medium severity

So what: Regulation here is a slow, persistent margin tax on the tenant, and therefore on the rent the landlord can negotiate.

R-09 Business as usual

Property tax step-up on sale

Trigger evidence
Proposition 13 caps assessment growth at 2%/year and resets at a change of ownership (X-09).
How it reaches the owner
A sale transfers the low Prop 13 basis to the buyer, who must re-underwrite a higher tax bill. That reduces what a buyer will pay. A hold keeps the low basis.
Mitigation
Quantify the buyer's post-sale tax step-up in the sale case; quantify the landlord's protected basis in the hold case. Where the basis is very low, holding has a real, measurable advantage.
Residual exposure
Certain if a sale occurs; severity set by the gap between basis and market value

So what: Prop 13 is one of the few structural reasons a long hold can beat a sale regardless of price.

R-10 Black swan

Wildfire: a regional event repricing the insurance market

Trigger evidence
The January 2025 Palisades Fire destroyed 6,845 structures, damaged 975 and killed 12 (Z-01 to Z-05).
How it reaches the owner
A central-LA drive-thru box is not in the wildland-urban interface, so direct destruction risk is low. The real channel is secondary: after a major fire, insurers reprice and withdraw across the whole region, and the FAIR Plan grows (Z-13). Reinsurance and construction costs rise region-wide.
Mitigation
Separate the two questions: (a) will this parcel burn, (b) will this parcel stay insurable and financeable after the region burns. (b) is the material one and it is not parcel-specific.
Residual exposure
Low probability of direct damage; medium probability of a regional repricing event within a 10-year hold

So what: The correct framing is insurance-market contagion, not ember exposure. Do not assign a burn probability to the parcel; do stress the post-event cost of cover.

R-11 Black swan

Earthquake: shaking, liquefaction or fault rupture

Trigger evidence
USGS/CGS ShakeOut scenario models 1,800 deaths and $191bn of economic loss for an Mw 7.8 San Andreas event (Z-09, Z-10).
How it reaches the owner
Central Los Angeles sits in a basin with strong shaking amplification. CGS maps liquefaction and landslide zones, and Alquist-Priolo fault zones impose a 50-foot setback (Z-08). A single-storey box is more resilient than mid-rise, but a closed store, a red-tagged parcel and a business-interruption gap still hit rent collection.
Mitigation
Query the CGS EQ Zapp and the Alquist-Priolo maps for the parcel (Z-15, F-21). Confirm the lease allocates repair obligation and whether rent abates on damage. Consider earthquake cover and its deductible.
Residual exposure
Low annual probability; high severity when it happens

So what: A 1,372 sq ft single-storey building is the favourable end of the exposure. The lease's damage and abatement language matters more than the building's structural risk.

R-12 Black swan

Flood and extreme heat

Trigger evidence
Los Angeles County heat and flood exposure is mapped by FEMA and the County; the parcel-level result is not verified here (Z-16).
How it reaches the owner
Flood damages the box and the parking field and interrupts trade. Heat raises refrigeration and HVAC cost and suppresses midday foot traffic - relevant because a drive-thru's peak is lunch.
Mitigation
Check the FEMA flood map and the County flood control viewer for the parcel. Confirm tenant obligation for HVAC replacement.
Residual exposure
Medium probability of heat events; low-to-medium severity for a single-storey box

So what: Heat is the under-modelled risk for a drive-thru: it attacks the lunch daypart and the equipment simultaneously.

R-13 Black swan

Civil unrest, crime or a localised demand shock

Trigger evidence
No official parcel-level crime or unrest series was verified for this site; the general Los Angeles context is cited on the market page.
How it reaches the owner
A single-event shock can close a store for weeks and accelerate a closure decision that was already pending. For a tenant already closing 50-100 units a year, a shock can be the trigger.
Mitigation
Confirm the lease's rent-abatement and casualty provisions. Confirm business-interruption cover belongs to the tenant, as it normally does under NNN.
Residual exposure
Low probability; medium severity

So what: For a tenant in contraction, the black swan does not need to be large - it only needs to coincide with a closure list.

R-14 Black swan

Regional economic recession

Trigger evidence
Restaurant demand is discretionary. The tenant's own 10-K names consumer confidence and food-away-from-home spending as drivers of its results (T-06, sec10K risk factors).
How it reaches the owner
A recession hits same-store sales, then franchisee profitability, then rent-paying capacity, then renewal terms. Under NNN the landlord's cash flow is contractual and therefore lags the shock by the lease term.
Mitigation
The lease term is the hedge. This is precisely the argument for a renewal: contractual rent through a downturn is worth more than a mark-to-market sale at the bottom.
Residual exposure
Medium probability within a 10-year horizon; medium severity while a lease is in place

So what: A recession argues for term, not for selling into weakness.

R-15 Black swan

Policy shock: new rent, transfer or business taxes

Trigger evidence
Measure ULA itself is the precedent: a city-level transfer tax introduced in 2023 and indexed since (X-01 to X-08).
How it reaches the owner
The measure that taxed this asset class once can be extended. A higher transfer tax directly reduces net sale proceeds; a new commercial rent tax would hit the income stream.
Mitigation
Nothing mitigates a future tax except timing and structure. Note that a sale now is taxed under known rules; a sale in 2033 is taxed under unknown ones.
Residual exposure
Low-to-medium probability; medium severity

So what: Policy risk cuts both ways and is not a reason to delay by itself - but it does argue against an open-ended hold with no exit plan.

R-16 Personal

The owner's own liquidity, estate or concentration need

Trigger evidence
Not observable and not requested. No personal financial information is collected or published.
How it reaches the owner
This is the factor that most often overrides a pure asset analysis: a need for cash, an estate event, a portfolio that is too concentrated in one tenant, or a desire to stop being a landlord.
Mitigation
Decide this first, privately. If liquidity is needed inside 36 months, the sale case dominates regardless of the market view.
Residual exposure
Owner-specific

So what: The asset analysis can tell the owner which path is better for the asset. Only the owner can say which path is better for the owner.
