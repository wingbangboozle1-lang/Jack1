What the numbers say

Employment is flat, and the growth is in the wrong sector for a burger chain

The metro added payrolls at +0.5% year on year. The only sector with real momentum is education and health services (+3.8%), which is salaried, daytime and least exposed to discretionary food spend. Information — the entertainment and tech base that drives lunch and late-night trade — is down 5.7%. Financial activities (−1.7%) and professional services (−0.5%) are also negative.

Implication: the daypart mix that a Jack in the Box depends on (breakfast, lunch, late night) is exposed to the two weakest parts of the local economy.

Costs are rising faster than menu prices can follow

All-items CPI is +3.7% but food away from home is only +2.8%, so restaurants are absorbing roughly 90bp of real price compression before they touch input costs. Energy is +20.3% and gasoline +30.8% year on year — a direct hit to drive-thru traffic and, through the NNN recovery, to the tenant's occupancy cost.

Implication: margin pressure lands on the tenant first and on the landlord at renewal, because a tenant with compressed margins cannot absorb a rent increase.

Money is expensive: 4.90% risk-free sets the floor

The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.90% on 4 November 2026 and traded as high as 5.31% in early October. A buyer of a single-tenant asset financed at that level needs either a long lease or a high cap rate. Both translate into a lower bid for a lease with six years left than for one with sixteen.

Implication: capital-markets conditions, not the neighbourhood, will set the sale price. That is an argument for selling into term, not for holding through it.

The market is large, which is why the exit exists at all

City-wide accommodation and food services sales were $17.37bn in the 2022 Economic Census; retail sales were $81.35bn, or $21,281 per capita. A dense, high-turnover commercial market is what gives a dark store a second life and what gives a buyer a reason to bid on land.

Implication: the downside from vacancy is bounded by re-leasing and redevelopment prospects — but only if the parcel's zoning supports it, which is unverified (U-04).

The single most important number on this page is not on it

The cap rate a buyer will apply to this asset is not published by any government agency. Rather than invent one, the projection model treats it as an explicit, adjustable assumption and shows the break-even at which holding stops making sense. That is recorded as open item U-06.
