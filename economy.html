Area economy · jobs, prices and cost of capital

Metropolitan area figures - not a trade area

Employment is flat-to-growing, the consumer price index is not, and money is not cheap.

Total nonfarm employment in the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Santa Ana metropolitan area grew 0.5% over the twelve months to September 2026, with unemployment at 4.4%. Los Angeles area consumer prices rose 3.7% over the year. The ten-year Treasury yields 4.84%. Each number pushes the sell-versus-renew answer in a different direction.

Reading: the metro added roughly 31,000 nonfarm jobs over the year (0.5% of 6.28 million). The mix matters more than the total: education and health services added 3.8%, while information shed 5.7% and financial activities 1.7%. A central Los Angeles quick-service site draws on office, health, education and visitor traffic, so a shrinking information sector is a headwind for weekday lunch and a growing health sector is a partial offset. The county's own unemployment rate is not published here because the BLS page read covers the metropolitan area (ECO-22).

Reading: food away from home rose 2.8% over the year while all items rose 3.7% and energy rose 20.3%. Restaurant menu prices are therefore rising more slowly than the household's overall basket, which squeezes real margins at the same time as California's $20.00 fast-food wage floor pushes costs up. For a landlord, this is the mechanism that turns into rent-coverage pressure at renewal.
