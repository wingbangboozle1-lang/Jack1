(p) = preliminary, per BLS footnotes.

**Read:** the metro is at ~full employment (4.4%) but *not adding jobs* — total nonfarm
is up only 0.5% year-over-year. This is the central tension in the whole analysis: a
tight-labour, low-growth economy. Tight labour = wage pressure on the tenant's P&L;
low growth = weak rent growth for the landlord.

## L3. Tenant labour cost — the structural risk to renewal

| Field | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California state minimum wage | **$16.90/hr** effective 2026-01-01 (was $16.50 in 2025) | https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_minimumwage.htm |
| Fast-food minimum wage (AB 1228) | **$20.00/hr**, effective 2024-04-01, applies to limited-service chains with ≥60 US locations, including franchisees | https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Fast-Food-Minimum-Wage-FAQ.htm |
| Statutory escalator | After reaching $15, state rate indexed to CPI-W, max +3.5%/yr, no decreases, Governor cannot pause | https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_minimumwage.htm (FAQ 2) |
| Fast Food Council power | Empowered to make further increases and adopt other minimum employment standards | https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Fast-Food-Minimum-Wage-FAQ.htm (FAQ 1) |

**Read:** the tenant's largest controllable cost is store labour, and in California it is
indexed upward by statute with a council that can add more. A Jack in the Box unit in LA
employs roughly 15–25 people (assumption — flagged, no official per-store headcount
series exists). At $20/hr plus payroll burden, labour is the swing factor in whether a
franchisee wants to renew.

**Flagged / unverified:** whether the $20.00 fast-food rate changed in 2026. The DIR FAQ
still states $20.00 (page read 2026-11-06). No Fast Food Council determination raising it
was found on a DIR page. Secondary commentary (not official) consistently reports "no
change in 2026" but that is *not* an official confirmation and is therefore published as
**unverified** on the site rather than asserted.
