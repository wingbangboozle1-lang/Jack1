# Executive Summary

**Subject:** single-tenant Jack in the Box restaurant, absolute-NNN lease, Central Los Angeles, lease expiry
window **2030–2035**. **Question:** sell before expiry, hold to expiry, or renew.
**Prepared:** 2026-11-06. **Anonymized:** no address, parcel, owner, tenant contact or lease financials appear
on this site or in this document.

## 1. The recommendation in one paragraph
**Negotiate a long renewal now, then choose whether to sell the re-rated asset or hold it.** The single
largest, known, and controllable loss in this deal is *lease-term decay*: every year that passes without a
renewal removes a year of contractual income from a buyer's underwriting, and buyers of single-tenant NNN
assets steeply discount terms under about five years. A renewal removes that discount and simultaneously
re-rates the asset into the deepest buyer pool. It costs only legal and negotiation time — no transfer tax, no
listing risk, and it keeps the sell option fully open. Selling today instead crystallises the shortest
remaining term of the entire hold and pays a seller-side city transfer tax of **4.45%** to do it. Holding to
expiry without a renewal keeps tenant-credit risk all the way to the end and then hands you vacancy,
tenant-improvement cost and downtime at the worst moment.

## 2. Why this is the risk-balanced answer, not just the highest-return one
- It converts an **irreversible** decision (sell now at the shortest term) into a **reversible** one
  (renew, then choose). That is what wealth preservation means in a single-asset portfolio.
- It does **not** require a forecast. If the market pays up after the renewal, you sell a better asset. If it
  does not, you simply do not list the property. You are never a forced seller.
- It removes the only risk on the register that you can actually control (lease-term decay) while leaving you
  exposed to the risks you cannot control (rates, tenant credit) for no longer than necessary.

## 4. What the official evidence says about the tenant
Jack in the Box Inc. (SEC CIK 0000807882) went from **net income of +$130.8M in FY2023 to −$36.7M in FY2024 and
−$80.7M in FY2025**, with revenue falling from $1,692.3M to $1,465.3M over the same period. In June 2026 it
raised **$500M of 7.624% senior secured notes** with an **anticipated repayment date of May 2031** — a
refinancing deadline that sits *inside* your lease-expiry window, secured partly by the franchise system's
real-estate assets.

## 7. Risk headline
Twelve risks are scored in the register. The four that matter most:

1. **Lease-term decay (controllable, certain).** The single largest driver of exit value. Solved by renewal.
2. **Cap-rate expansion (uncontrollable, high likelihood).** Mitigated by speed after renewal, and by not being
   a forced seller.
3. **Tenant credit (uncontrollable, high severity).** Mitigated by guaranty/LOC and by checking whether this is
   a corporate or franchisee lease.
4. **Tail risks** — wildfire (CAL FIRE 2025 LRA hazard maps), earthquake/surface fault (Alquist-Priolo zones),
   and a credit seizure that closes the exit. A renewal-and-hold posture is the only structure that survives
   all three without a forced sale.

## 8. What is still unknown (and deliberately not guessed)
Exact lease dates and rent, in-place NOI, appraised value, the parcel's fire and seismic zone classification,
insurance premium, issuer credit rating, and store-level sales. **All twelve items are published on the
"Flagged for Review" page with a resolution path** and none of them changes the *direction* of the
recommendation — only its magnitude.

## 9. Decision rule, stated plainly
- Tenant will sign a 10-year extension at acceptable rent → **renew, then sell into strength in 2027–2029.**
- Tenant refuses a renewal on acceptable terms → **sell now** into the current liquid market.
- Tenant refuses any renewal → **sell now**; do not hold to expiry.
- You need cash within 12 months → **sell now** and start the 1031 identification immediately.
- A recession or credit event is clearly underway → **do not list**; hold the renewed asset, because you are
  not forced to sell.
