Discovery plan

How to close the blanks without guessing

  1. Title and survey first. Title commitment, ALTA survey, and the Assessor parcel page establish the address, APN, vesting, easements, and liens. Nothing else can be matched to the right parcel until this is done.
  2. Read the lease, not the label. Executed lease, all amendments, guaranty, side letters, and estoppel. Calendar every option, notice, and estoppel date into a single tracker with reminders.
  3. Cash-flow evidence. Rent roll, 24–36 months of operating statements, bank statements, tax and insurance bills, and the CAM reconciliations — these convert a stated NNN label into actual net income.
  4. Credit file. Legal tenant and guarantor identity, financial statements, franchise agreement, and payment/default history. Compare against the issuer's quarterly filings already published here.
  5. Asset condition. Property condition assessment, roof and HVAC reports, seismic evaluation, Phase I environmental, and a capital reserve study.
  6. Insurance. Current policy and endorsements, three years of loss runs, and two broker indications including a FAIR Plan fallback with an honest replacement-cost estimate.
  7. Value. Appraisal plus two broker opinions of value, and a market test at a real asking price. Compare executable net proceeds against risk-adjusted hold value.
  8. Tax and financing. Basis, depreciation schedule, entity structure, marginal rates, exchange eligibility, payoff statement, prepayment penalties, and lender term sheets.
  9. Market evidence for the trade area. Parcel-keyed 1/3/5-mile demographics, competitor and traffic counts, and the reporting division's multi-year crime series — replacing the citywide and county proxies published on the Market page.
