The ULA cliff. The City of Los Angeles charges a 0.45% base real property transfer tax. Measure ULA adds 4.0% on consideration above $5,400,000 and 5.5% at $10,900,000 or more, and the surcharge applies to the whole price, not the excess. On a sale at exactly $5,400,000 the city tax is $24,300; one dollar higher it is $240,300. Working the other way, at a 6.25% capitalisation rate a $5,400,000 price corresponds to $337,500 of annual net operating income - so whether a sale crosses the line is a question about rent, not about negotiation style.

Assessment and wages. Proposition 13 caps assessed-value growth at 2% a year, and Proposition 8 allows a temporary decline-in-value reduction filed between 2 July and 30 November (REG-06, REG-07). California's fast-food minimum wage has been $20.00 an hour since 1 April 2024, which is a direct cost line for the tenant and therefore a rent-coverage risk for the landlord (REG-08). Unverified regulatory items - the citywide minimum wage, county documentary transfer tax and the parcel's own tax rate - are blank (REG-10 to REG-12).

Diligence

The five documents that decide this question

In priority order:

  1. The executed lease and every amendment. Firm term, expiry date, renewal options and notice windows, rent schedule, percentage rent, NNN exclusions, roof and structure, assignment and change-of-control, guaranty and recourse.
  2. Two years of rent ledger and NNN reconciliations. What was actually billed and collected, and what the tenant disputed.
  3. Current title, survey and the assessor's parcel record. Vesting, easements, liens, access and the Prop 13 base year value.
  4. Insurance policy, loss runs and quotes. Coverage, deductibles, exclusions, business-interruption terms and what the lease makes the tenant carry.
  5. A broker's opinion of value with three closed net-lease comparables inside central Los Angeles, plus the tenant's most recent quarterly filing.
