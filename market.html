Market fundamentals and the population outlook to 2035

A restaurant lease is a claim on a catchment area's disposable spending. This page tests that catchment with the two most authoritative series available: the U.S. Census Bureau's official estimates and the California Department of Finance's official projections.

What the numbers say

The catchment is contracting, officially

Los Angeles County lost 3.2% of its residents between April 2020 and July 2025 — about 322,000 people on the published base. The state's own projection has the county at 9.73m in 2030 and 9.71m in 2035, i.e. another 1.5% decline across the exact window in which this lease expires. California as a whole is projected to grow over the same period, so this is a share shift away from Los Angeles, not a state-level story.

Implication: do not underwrite unit-volume growth. Every dollar of projected rent growth should come from contractual escalation, not from more customers.

Income supports a value-led QSR, not a premium one

Median household income of $81,939 against a median home value of $921,200 and median gross rent of $1,933 leaves little discretionary slack; 16.5% of residents are below the poverty line and the owner-occupancy rate is 36.0%, so the trade area is renter-dominated and highly price-sensitive. Food-away-from-home inflation is running at 2.8%, below the 3.7% all-items rate.

Implication: a $20/hour statutory wage floor plus a price-sensitive catchment is the hardest combination in food service. It caps what the tenant can pay at renewal.

Housing supply is responding, slowly

22,250 building permits were issued county-wide in 2025 against 3.73m housing units, with 8,304 people per square mile in the city. Density plus permit flow is what creates redevelopment optionality for a 1,372 sq ft prototype on a commercially-zoned parcel.

Implication: the residual land value may exceed the going-concern restaurant value if the lease lapses. That is a floor, not a strategy.

Two official population series disagree — by design

Census puts the county at 9,694,934 for 2025; the California Department of Finance's Baseline-2024 projection puts it at 9,853,435 for the same year, a gap of 158,501. They are different products with different methods and different vintages. Both are published here as separate rows and never blended.

This is irregularity R-01 in the verification log. It does not change the direction of travel: both series have the county flat-to-declining through 2035.

What this page does to the decision

It removes the growth case for holding. If the catchment is flat-to-shrinking and incomes are stretched, a renewal can only be justified by the tenant's own performance or by redevelopment value — not by the neighbourhood getting better. That narrows the hold case to two narrow conditions, which the decision page tests.
